# Independent ARR Review — Experimental Methodology, IR Evaluation, and Statistics

## Paper summary and claimed contributions

This paper studies LLM query expansion for hybrid dense–sparse retrieval. It argues that evaluation after truncating each channel at a fixed top-$L$ mixes the effect of query construction with the effect of the chosen fusion depth. It therefore evaluates effectiveness using complete-list weighted reciprocal-rank fusion (WRRF), then replays incremental access and records the dense and sparse prefix depths at which the complete-fusion ordered top-20 can be certified. The proposed DESA method uses one set of complementary generated passages asymmetrically: an orthogonal residual is added to the dense query representation, while the sparse score is the product of the original-query and expanded-query BM25 scores. The claimed contributions are (i) a cutoff-sensitivity argument and exact-replay evaluation protocol, (ii) the channel-asymmetric DESA construction, and (iii) evidence across seven benchmarks, several controlled ablations, prior-method reproductions, a second generator and dense encoder, and corpus-scale variants.

The central controlled result is promising: on the seven-dataset equal-weight macro in Table 2, DESA improves nDCG@10 from .4572 to .4747 and Recall@20 from .4586 to .4695 relative to no expansion, while its replay depths are lower. The manuscript is unusually candid that the access result is not universal (Section 5.5 and the Conclusion). However, I do not yet find the access-depth interpretation, cutoff claim, statistical reporting, and some comparative claims sufficiently established for ACL Main.

## Strengths

1. **The controlled integration comparison is well conceived.** Section 4.3 holds DESA's generated passages fixed between shared expansion and DESA, so the comparison isolates integration more cleanly than a comparison between unrelated prompts and generators. The $2\times2$ operator experiment in Section 5.3 is also the right basic design for separating dense residual expansion and sparse anchoring.

2. **Score-product anchoring has a clear, testable mechanism.** Section 3.3.2 states the non-negative scoring assumption and makes the lexical-support property explicit. The binary-mask ablation in Section 5.3 usefully distinguishes preservation of support from preservation of the graded original-query score.

3. **The empirical scope is broad for an experimental IR paper.** Section 4.2 includes seven datasets spanning several domains and task types. Section 5.5 reports a second generator, a second dense encoder, nested corpus sizes, and an adverse Contriever/Touché-2020 case rather than presenting only favorable results.

4. **Several reproducibility details are excellent.** Appendix A gives model revisions, decoding parameters, deterministic seed construction, validation/fallback behavior, encoder revisions, analyzer and BM25 parameters, tie-breaking, and artifact hashes. The exact complete-fusion target and the distinction between logical rank access and latency are stated explicitly in Sections 4.1 and 4.5.

5. **The main comparison against no expansion is supported by paired analysis.** Section 5.2 reports Holm-adjusted results for both effectiveness metrics and both replay depths, and the frozen paired-results CSV confirms nonzero bootstrap intervals for all four favorable differences. The paper also correctly avoids claiming that DESA dominates shared expansion on access: Table 2 shows shared expansion has smaller mean depths.

## Acceptance-critical weaknesses

1. **The reported depth pair is an algorithm-dependent replay outcome, not yet shown to be an intrinsic or cost-optimal “depth at which the result becomes fixed.”** In Section 3.4 and Appendix A.5, the replay always reads the channel with larger next-rank RRF bound. With equal weights and the same WRRF constant, $u_D(l)=u_S(l)$ whenever the prefix lengths are equal, so the rule alternates channels (Dense wins a tie) until exhaustion or stopping. This mechanical coupling is visible in Tables 2 and 3, where $L_D$ and $L_S$ are often nearly equal. Consequently, “both depths decrease” and two separate depth tests are largely redundant except when sparse support exhausts; changing either channel's ranking can change the global certificate and hence both reported depths. More importantly, no proof is given that this greedy schedule minimizes total rank accesses, either channel's accesses, or a stated cost. Other schedules can yield different sufficient pairs, so phrases such as “the per-channel depths at which ... becomes fixed” (Abstract; Sections 1, 3.4, and 6) currently overstate what is measured. The paper should define the access primitive and objective, prove the greedy policy optimal for that objective or label the numbers as policy-specific replay costs, and compare against at least one alternative policy or report a minimal-access/Pareto certificate. A total-access measure and per-channel measures should not be treated as four independent outcomes without discussing their dependence.

2. **The fixed-top-$L$ argument conflates an operational evaluation choice with a statistical confound, and the evidence for changed conclusions is too weak.** Table 1 does establish that truncated and complete fusion can produce different rankings. It does not establish that fixed $L$ is inherently invalid: when the same specified $L$ is applied to all methods, it defines a legitimate deployed retrieval procedure rather than an uncontrolled confound. The two headline reversals in Section 5.1 are extremely small (.4641 versus .4639, and .4537 versus .4489 becoming .4565 versus .4572), with no paired uncertainty or significance analysis. Exact top-20 disagreement also shows ranking sensitivity, not that an effectiveness conclusion changes reliably. The paper should soften “remove this confound” (Section 1), frame complete-list fusion as a complementary estimand, and provide paired query-level uncertainty for method-difference changes across $L$ (ideally including rank-correlation and the frequency/magnitude of statistically meaningful reversals). Otherwise the strongest motivation rests on possibly negligible crossings.

3. **Statistical reporting is incomplete and the inferential family is underspecified.** Section 4.5 says 95% stratified-bootstrap intervals are reported, but no confidence intervals appear in Tables 1–3 or elsewhere in the manuscript. Section 5.2 gives selected adjusted $p$-values but not the full primary comparison table; notably, the frozen paired results show that against shared expansion only nDCG is significant, while Recall@20 and both depths are not. Holm correction is applied separately to four outcomes for each of two declared primary comparisons, although the rationale for treating the two comparison families separately is not given. In addition, three stochastic generation runs are averaged within query before inference, so the reported intervals reflect query sampling but do not expose generation-run variability. Please report all primary effect estimates, intervals, raw and adjusted $p$-values; define the family before correction (or correct the eight declared primary tests together); and use or justify a hierarchical query/run analysis. Dataset-level paired effects are also needed because the equal-dataset macro combines very different query counts and the access distributions appear strongly skewed.

4. **The paper claims a contribution from complementary evidence generation without isolating it.** Sections 1 and 4.4 say the controlled experiments examine complementary generation, orthogonal residual expansion, and score-product anchoring, but Section 5.3 only isolates the two integration operators and varies the number of references. The prior-method comparison changes prompts, passage length/count, and integration simultaneously. Thus no experiment shows that the complementary, intent-preserving prompt is better than a standard or non-complementary multi-reference prompt under the same DESA operators and generation budget. Add this prompt-level control, along with generation quality/failure statistics, or narrow the contribution and causal interpretation to channel integration.

5. **The prior-method comparison is not yet a fidelity-quality baseline comparison.** Section 5.4 calls this the “full-method comparison” using published strategies, but Appendix A.3 states that MuGI's pseudo-relevance-feedback calibration is omitted. Generation budgets also differ substantially (e.g., HyDE uses eight texts up to 512 tokens, Query2doc one document up to 120 words, and DESA five one-sentence passages), which may be appropriate for fidelity but does not yield a budget-controlled comparison. The reason all three baselines are available only on FiQA, ArguAna, Touché-2020, and SCIDOCS is not explained, and there are no paired tests in Table 3; Query2doc is in fact higher on nDCG. Either reproduce full published pipelines and explain the common-subset selection, or label the methods as partial/adapted reproductions and add a matched-generation-budget comparison. Comparative claims against MuGI and HyDE should be qualified until then.

6. **At least two access-ablation claims do not match the frozen numerical evidence.** Section 5.3 reports dense-only reductions of 8.85%/9.82% and sparse-only reductions of 24.70%/24.17%. The frozen equal-dataset access summary (`report/access-macro-bootstrap.csv`) gives 8.16%/7.49% for dense-only and 28.77%/27.74% for sparse-only. These are not rounding differences. Because the paper's mechanism interpretation relies on these numbers, all manuscript values should be regenerated from one frozen table pipeline and audited before acceptance.

## Non-critical weaknesses and clarifications

1. **Dataset specification is too thin.** Section 4.2 does not report query counts, corpus sizes, qrel versions, dataset releases, or exclusions. These details are needed to reconstruct the exact seven-task macro and to interpret small datasets such as Touché-2020 and TREC-COVID.

2. **Hyperparameter selection and the claimed held-out status are unclear.** Equal weights and $\kappa=60$ are fixed in Section 4.1, and five references are selected, but the development protocol is not described. Appendix A.6 refers to “held-out evaluation” without naming a development set or stating which design choices were frozen when. Section 4.4 promises a $\kappa\in\{2,20,60,100\}$ analysis, but Section 5 does not report it. This should be supplied to rule out selection on the evaluation benchmarks.

3. **Mean depth alone is not robust for heavy-tailed costs.** Table 2's large means and wide frozen bootstrap intervals indicate strong skew. Report medians and upper quantiles, per-dataset distributions, and the proportion of increases as primary descriptive statistics. The 63.31% dual-improvement rate is helpful but should be accompanied by the magnitude of regressions among the remaining queries.

4. **Complete-list fusion needs a sharper candidate-universe definition in the main text.** Section 3.4 says a missing rank contributes zero, while Appendix A.5 discusses exhausted sparse support. Please state explicitly whether zero-BM25 documents are absent rather than tied at the end, how dense coverage is guaranteed, and how complete targets are computed when a channel has finite support.

5. **The mechanism interpretation should acknowledge interactions.** From the $2\times2$ results in Section 5.3, the dense and sparse operators alter one fused certificate jointly; the statement that sparse anchoring “accounts for most” of quality and the residual “further reduces” evidence is descriptive, not a channel-local causal decomposition. Report the interaction contrast with uncertainty.

6. **Efficiency claims should remain explicitly logical.** Section 4.5 correctly says replay depth is not wall-clock latency. The Abstract's “reducing dense and sparse access depths,” however, may be read as retrieval-time savings even though full rankings were computed offline and the manuscript does not show an index capable of exact incremental access with the assumed costs. A brief operational discussion and latency/memory experiment would strengthen impact, though I do not require one for the ranking-method claim.

## Strongest plausible rejection argument

The strongest rejection case is that the paper's most distinctive evaluation contribution is not yet validated as a meaningful access metric. The stopping pair depends on a particular greedy replay policy whose optimality and system realization are not shown; with equal-weight WRRF the policy mechanically keeps the two depths nearly synchronized, making “both-channel” reductions and separate channel tests partly artifacts of the schedule. At the same time, the argument that fixed-$L$ evaluation changes substantive conclusions is based on tiny, untested reversals, while complete-list fusion is itself a different, potentially non-operational estimand. If those two points are not repaired, the paper remains an interesting query-expansion method evaluated with a bespoke logical metric rather than a demonstrated advance in hybrid-retrieval evaluation.

## Changes most likely to raise the score by at least 0.5

1. Formalize the replay objective and prove the greedy rule optimal, or report policy-specific total cost plus a minimal/Pareto certificate and sensitivity to alternative schedules. Explain the induced dependence between $L_D$ and $L_S$.
2. Recast complete-list and fixed-$L$ fusion as two estimands, and add paired uncertainty for method differences across $L$ rather than relying on two tiny macro crossings.
3. Add a compact complete primary-statistics table with effect sizes, 95% CIs, all adjusted tests, per-dataset effects, and generation-run variability; correct the multiplicity family or justify it.
4. Add a prompt-level complementary-generation ablation with identical DESA integration and a matched generation budget.
5. Reconcile every reported percentage with the frozen CSV pipeline, document development/held-out choices, and report the promised WRRF-constant analysis.
6. Either complete the published baseline pipelines on a justified common set or clearly relabel partial reproductions and add budget-controlled variants.

## Scores

- **Soundness: 3/5.** The main no-expansion effectiveness result is credible and the method is clearly defined, but the access metric's interpretation/optimality, incomplete uncertainty reporting, baseline fidelity, and numerical inconsistencies are material.
- **Excitement: 3/5.** Channel-asymmetric integration is simple and useful, and the complete-target/replay perspective is thought-provoking, but the present evidence does not yet establish the broader evaluation claim or operational impact.
- **Overall assessment: 2.5/5 (between resubmit and Findings).** I would not recommend ACL Main in the current form. A revision that formalizes replay cost and repairs the statistical and ablation evidence could become a solid Findings paper and potentially approach borderline Main if the cutoff/access thesis survives those tests.
- **Reviewer confidence: 4/5.** I am confident in the IR-evaluation and statistical concerns after checking the manuscript's frozen summary tables; uncertainty remains about implementation details and any artifact material not described in the paper.

**One-line verdict: resubmit.**
