# Independent ARR Review — Novelty and Conceptual Contribution

## Paper summary and claimed contributions

This paper studies LLM-based query expansion for hybrid dense–sparse retrieval. It argues that conventional fixed top-$L$ fusion confounds the quality of a query construction with the chosen channel cutoff. The paper therefore evaluates quality using complete-list weighted reciprocal-rank fusion (WRRF) and measures the dense and sparse prefix depths required to certify the same ordered top-20. Its proposed method, DESA, generates complementary pseudo-reference passages and integrates them asymmetrically: an orthogonal residual of the generated dense representations is added to the original query vector (Section 3, Eq. 4), while original-query and expanded-query BM25 scores are multiplied document-wise to preserve original lexical support (Section 3, Eq. 7). Across seven benchmarks, DESA improves the equal-dataset macro nDCG@10/Recall@20 over no expansion while reducing logical replay depths; it is also compared with shared expansion and, on four datasets, HyDE, MuGI, and Query2doc.

The paper claims three contributions: (1) demonstrating that fixed top-$L$ can change measured query-expansion gains and even method ordering (Section 5.1, Table 1); (2) introducing channel-asymmetric dense residual expansion and sparse score-product anchoring (Sections 3.2–3.3); and (3) evaluating retrieval effectiveness jointly with exact per-channel replay depth, including operator, generator, encoder, and corpus-scale analyses (Sections 5.2–5.5).

## Strengths

1. **The paper identifies a real evaluation confound and illustrates it concretely.** Section 5.1 and Table 1 show two actual method-order reversals between fixed-depth and complete-list fusion, while the very low exact top-20 agreement at small $L$ makes the issue tangible. This is a useful warning for hybrid-retrieval evaluation, even though the complete-list/replay machinery itself is inherited from EAHR (Section 2.3).

2. **The channel-asymmetric design is intuitively well matched to dense and sparse retrieval.** The dense operator in Section 3.3 removes the component of generated evidence parallel to the original query, and the sparse operator guarantees that documents supported only by generated terms receive zero anchored score. The slogan “Dense expands; Sparse anchors” accurately summarizes the mechanism and is easy to remember.

3. **The controlled shared-expansion comparison is well chosen.** Section 4.3 holds generated passages constant and changes only channel integration. This is more diagnostic than comparing only against separately prompted prior systems. The NFCorpus result in Section 5.2 is especially useful: shared expansion increases sparse replay depth by 105.82%, whereas score-product anchoring reduces it by 23.15%.

4. **The empirical scope is broader than that of many query-expansion papers.** The study spans seven benchmarks, three generation draws, two generators, two dense encoders, multiple WRRF constants, reference-count and sparse-mask ablations, and nested TREC-COVID corpora (Sections 4.2, 4.4, and 5.5). The Contriever/Touché-2020 failure case is disclosed rather than hidden (Section 5.5).

5. **Reproducibility details are unusually strong.** The appendix specifies model revisions, decoding, deterministic seed construction, validation/fallback behavior, BM25 parameters, tie-breaking, replay bounds, and artifact hashes (Appendix A.1–A.6). The statistical protocol also uses paired randomization and Holm correction for the designated primary comparisons (Section 4.5).

6. **The topic is relevant to ACL.** The work concerns how LLM-generated language should be integrated into retrieval, connects generation behavior to lexical and semantic matching, and evaluates a language-model-based IR method. It would interest ACL readers working on retrieval-augmented NLP and generative information access, although the contribution is also strongly IR-oriented.

## Acceptance-critical weaknesses

1. **The paper does not yet establish a Main-level conceptual novelty gap.** The evaluation contribution is explicitly an application of EAHR’s already published complete-ranking target and replay procedure (Section 2.3, lines 58–64; Section 3.4). The remaining novelty is Eq. 4’s elementary vector projection and Eq. 7’s document-wise multiplication. Simplicity is not a problem, but Section 2.2 gives only a one-paragraph verbal distinction—“couples intent-preserving generation with dense expansion and sparse anchoring”—rather than explaining how the operators relate to classic query-drift control, Rocchio/projection-style vector updates, lexical term reweighting, conjunctive filtering/gating, or score combination. With only ten bibliography entries, the literature positioning is too narrow to support the claim that the construction is conceptually new rather than a sensible recombination of known devices. This is the main reason I do not currently see a clear ACL Main contribution.

2. **The sparse ablations do not isolate why score multiplication is the right or novel operator.** Section 5.3 compares $s_o(d)s_r(d)$ only with a binary original-support mask. That establishes that retaining the magnitude of $s_o$ helps beyond a Boolean gate, but it does not establish that a product is superior to straightforward alternatives that also retain this magnitude: a tuned or normalization-free weighted sum within the original support, a log-space sum, rank-level combination, or an exponentiated/geometric mixture. Because BM25 scales depend on query length and term composition, multiplication can also amplify original-query scores in a scale-specific way. Without these controls, most of DESA’s measured quality gain—attributed to sparse anchoring in Section 5.3—could come from generic rescoring rather than the proposed principle.

3. **The prior-method evidence does not decisively support impact beyond Findings.** Table 3 evaluates HyDE, MuGI, and Query2doc only on four datasets, omits direct comparisons with the more closely related integration methods discussed in Section 2.2 (Word2Passage, Exp4Fuse, and DVCQR), and reports no significance tests for these comparisons. DESA is essentially tied with Query2doc on effectiveness: Query2doc is 0.45% better in nDCG, while DESA is 0.57% better in Recall (Section 5.4). Moreover, the appendix states that all baselines use the same primary generator and that MuGI is reproduced without its pseudo-relevance-feedback calibration (Appendix A.3), so “full-method comparison” is not fully faithful to every published system. The replay-depth advantage may still be valuable, but it is a logical certificate-depth metric inherited from EAHR rather than an end-to-end efficiency result.

4. **The manuscript promises, but does not show, enough disaggregated uncertainty to audit generalization.** Section 4.5 says results are reported for each dataset and with 95% bootstrap confidence intervals. In the supplied manuscript, Tables 2 and 3 show only equal-dataset macro point estimates; no per-dataset main table or confidence interval is given. Section 5.2 highlights one NFCorpus behavior, and Section 5.5 gives ranges, but these do not reveal whether gains are consistent, whether macro results are dominated by particular datasets, or how often quality degrades. This matters especially because the abstract generalizes “across seven benchmarks,” while the paper itself reports an encoder–dataset access failure. The missing per-dataset and uncertainty evidence is an acceptance-critical reporting gap, not a request for an unrelated new experiment.

5. **Development and evaluation separation is unclear.** The introduction says an “NFCorpus analysis” motivates the sparse design (Section 1, lines 31–40), and the same NFCorpus benchmark is included in the seven-dataset primary evaluation. Sections 4.4–4.5 vary reference count and WRRF constants on those datasets, but do not say which data informed the prompt, choice of five references, score product, projection coefficient, or final hyperparameters. Appendix A.6 says artifacts were frozen “before held-out evaluation,” yet no held-out split or development collection is identified. If design choices were selected after viewing these same test outcomes, the primary $p$-values do not account for that selection. The authors should explicitly document the development protocol and either identify truly held-out evaluations or qualify the inferential claims.

6. **The access-depth contribution is useful but its practical meaning is underspecified.** Section 4.5 correctly states that $L_D$ and $L_S$ are logical replay depths, not wall-clock latency. DESA additionally requires LLM generation, five query-reference dense encodings per draw, and two BM25 scoring passes before multiplication (Sections 3.2–3.3 and 4.2). Complete-list fusion/replay is evaluated from stored rankings (Appendix A.6). Therefore the reported 36% depth reductions do not by themselves show lower retrieval cost or deployability. The paper should either demonstrate an executable lazy retrieval setting where these certified depths translate into saved work, or more sharply frame the result as a structural concentration/certificate measure and avoid allowing it to carry the practical-impact argument for Main.

## Non-critical weaknesses

1. **The dense mechanism needs more analysis of geometry and query drift.** Eq. 4 gives the original unit vector and the residual an implicit fixed relative weight, while the reference average’s norm and the concatenation encoder behavior determine the actual rotation. Orthogonality does not itself establish intent preservation. Reporting residual norms/angles and an interpolation baseline would make the mechanism claim more convincing.

2. **Generation uncertainty is only partially represented.** Section 4.5 averages three generations within each query and then resamples queries. This is reasonable for query-level inference but does not characterize sensitivity to generation randomness. Per-run variance or a hierarchical analysis would help, especially where the Mistral robustness study uses at most 100 queries per dataset (Section 4.2).

3. **The fixed-$L$ reversal examples are numerically tiny.** The reversals in Section 5.1 are real but involve differences such as .4641 vs. .4639 and .4565 vs. .4572. Their statistical/practical stability is not reported. The much stronger exact-top-20 disagreement supports the evaluation point, but method-order claims should include uncertainty or counts of robust reversals across datasets and metrics.

4. **The macro depth presentation is easy to misread.** Table 2’s raw macro depths imply a much larger ratio reduction than the 36.90%/36.56% numbers in the prose because the latter average within-dataset percentage reductions. The text explains this, but the caption or table should display the aggregation definition directly and provide confidence intervals.

5. **Limitations are scattered rather than synthesized.** Section 5.5 and the conclusion mention the Contriever failure and non-monotonic scale effect, but the manuscript does not systematically discuss hallucinated or off-intent generated passages, generator cost, English-only evaluation, incomplete relevance judgments, or the restriction to WRRF and top-20 certification.

## Strongest plausible rejection argument

The strongest rejection case is that the paper’s apparent breadth masks an incremental core: it applies an existing complete-list/replay framework to query expansion and combines a standard vector residualization idea with a simple support gate plus score multiplication. The related-work section does not establish novelty against the most relevant classic and contemporary alternatives, the key product operator lacks strong functional baselines, and the only direct prior-method table shows near-parity with Query2doc on effectiveness on four datasets. Because disaggregated results, confidence intervals, and development/test separation are also absent from the manuscript, the current evidence does not justify elevating this useful engineering insight to ACL Main.

## Concrete changes that could raise the overall score by at least 0.5

1. Expand Section 2 into a precise novelty map: separate what is inherited from EAHR, what is newly proposed for dense integration, what is newly proposed for sparse integration, and how each differs mathematically from classic query-drift control, Rocchio/projection updates, gated/conjunctive sparse retrieval, term reweighting, and recent LLM expansion/fusion methods.

2. Add product-specific sparse controls that preserve original lexical support and score magnitude: masked weighted/additive fusion, geometric mixtures with exponents, rank-based fusion, and calibrated normalization. Pair these with residual-weight/interpolation controls for Dense. Report whether the exact parameter-free forms remain Pareto-preferred in quality and depth.

3. Add a compact per-dataset table (or appendix table) with nDCG@10, Recall@20, $L_D$, $L_S$, both-depth-decrease rate, confidence intervals, and paired differences for DESA vs. no expansion/shared expansion. Report uncertainty or significance for the prior-method comparisons as well.

4. Clarify all design-selection decisions and name the development data. If NFCorpus or the seven benchmark test sets informed the method, add a genuinely untouched evaluation set or reframe the tests as descriptive rather than confirmatory.

5. Either implement the replay with a realistic lazy dense/sparse retrieval interface and report end-to-end cost/latency, including generation, or explicitly demote practical efficiency claims and present replay depth as a certificate/concentration diagnostic. The former would materially strengthen the Main-conference impact case.

6. Compare directly with at least the closest available channel-aware or ranking-level expansion baselines cited in Section 2.2, under both faithful published configurations and the controlled common-generator setup. Clearly separate fidelity comparisons from mechanism-controlled comparisons.

## Scores

- **Soundness: 3.5 / 5.** The method is coherent, the support-preservation claim is correct under the stated non-negative scorer, and the controlled experiments are generally well designed. The score is limited by missing per-dataset/CI evidence, unclear selection protocol, and incomplete operator controls.
- **Excitement: 3.0 / 5.** The channel-asymmetry idea and cutoff diagnostic are useful and memorable, but the conceptual advance over prior retrieval machinery is currently modest and insufficiently differentiated.
- **Overall assessment: 3.0 / 5 (Findings).** I would support Findings in the current form. Main would require a clearer novelty case plus stronger evidence that the proposed operators, rather than generic gated rescoring and inherited replay evaluation, deliver a broadly important advance.
- **Reviewer confidence: 4.0 / 5.** I am confident in the IR/query-expansion and evaluation assessment. Confidence is not 5 because the manuscript does not provide the underlying per-dataset tables and because exact novelty could shift with a more complete account of contemporaneous 2026–2027 work.

## One-line verdict

**Findings.**
