\section{Introduction}
\label{sec:introduction}

Existing LLM-based query expansion methods augment the original query with
generated content and typically evaluate retrieval gains at a preset top-$L$
cutoff
\citep{gao-etal-2023-precise,wang-etal-2023-query2doc,zhang-etal-2024-exploring-best,liu-zhang-2025-exp4fuse}.
In hybrid retrieval, however, top-$L$ jointly determines how much of each
ranking is read and which documents contribute to fusion; changing $L$ may
therefore change the fused result itself \citep{zhang2026exact}. A gain measured
at a single cutoff is thus conditional on $L$ and does not isolate the effect
of query expansion.

To separate these factors, we first determine the fused result of each query
construction from the complete dense and sparse rankings
\citep{zhang2026exact}. A fixed sound replay procedure then reveals the two
rankings progressively and records the per-channel depths at which the same
ordered top-$K$ is certified. This separates two questions: what fused result
does an expansion produce, and how much ranked evidence is required to
determine it? The answers need not move together. An expansion may improve
retrieval effectiveness while pushing decisive evidence deeper into one
channel. We therefore ask whether query expansion can improve effectiveness
while reducing the access depth required from both channels.

Expansion effects are retriever-dependent
\citep{weller-etal-2024-generative,li-etal-2026-dvcqr}. On NFCorpus, applying
the same expansion to both channels improves the fused result and reduces dense
access depth, but increases sparse access depth. Generated terms substantially
enlarge the set of documents with nonzero sparse scores, preventing the sparse
ranking from exhausting as early as it does for the original query. This
reversal motivates an asymmetric design: dense retrieval should absorb new
semantic evidence, whereas sparse retrieval should restrict it to documents
already supported by the original query.

We address this asymmetry with DESA (Dense Expansion and Sparse Anchoring), a
channel-asymmetric query expansion method for hybrid retrieval. Given an
original query, the LLM generates several complementary reference passages
around the same information need. For dense retrieval, orthogonal residual
expansion preserves the original query direction and adds only the new semantic
component supplied by the references. For sparse retrieval, score-product
anchoring multiplies each document's scores under the original and expanded
queries. Documents matched only by generated terms receive no anchored score,
while the new terms may still reorder documents supported by the original
query. Together, these operations broaden dense semantic coverage without
broadening sparse support: Dense expands; sparse anchors.
Figure~\ref{fig:desa-intuition} illustrates this division of labor through two
retrieval examples.

Across seven public retrieval benchmarks, DESA improves equal-dataset macro
nDCG@10 and Recall@20 over the unexpanded query by 3.82\% and 2.38\%, while
reducing dense and sparse exact-access depths by 36.90\% and 36.56\%,
respectively. With equal dataset weighting, 63.31\% of queries become shallower
in both channels. Mechanism experiments show that sparse anchoring contributes
most of the effectiveness gain, while the dense orthogonal residual further
reduces the ranked evidence required to determine the fused result.
