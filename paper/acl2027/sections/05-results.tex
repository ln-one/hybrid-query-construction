\subsection{Fixed Cutoffs Alter Measured Effects}

Figure~\ref{fig:fixed-l-diagnostics} compares the query-level judgment of DESA
relative to Original at $L=50$ with the judgment under complete-list fusion.
Each query--draw pair is labeled as a gain, tie, or loss from the corresponding
metric difference. Diagonal cells preserve that judgment; off-diagonal cells
show cases in which the fixed cutoff changes it.

\begin{figure}[H]
\centering
\includegraphics[width=\columnwidth]{figures/fig_fixed_top_l_diagnostics.pdf}
\caption{Transition between the DESA-versus-Original judgment at fixed
$L=50$ (rows) and under complete-list fusion (columns). Cells report the
equal-dataset percentage of query--draw pairs. Green off-diagonal cells change
among gain, tie, and loss; red cells are strict gain--loss reversals.}
\label{fig:fixed-l-diagnostics}
\end{figure}

The fixed cutoff changes 6.95\% of nDCG judgments and 18.84\% of Recall
judgments. Strict gain--loss reversals account for 3.07\% and 3.06\%,
respectively. Thus, a practical fixed cutoff can change whether expansion is
judged beneficial for an individual query, rather than merely perturbing the
ranking. Results for other cutoffs and ordered top-20 agreement are reported in
Appendix Table~\ref{tab:cutoff-query-diagnostics}.

\subsection{Retrieval Effectiveness and Access Depth}

Table~\ref{tab:main-results} summarizes the controlled comparison. Under
complete-list fusion, DESA obtains the highest macro nDCG@10 and
Recall@20 in the seven-dataset controlled comparison, reaching .4747 and
.4695. Relative to Original, these values correspond to gains of 3.82\% and
2.38\%. The equal-dataset averages of the within-dataset replay stopping-depth
reductions are 36.90\% and 36.56\% for the dense and sparse channels. The mean
proportion of queries that become shallower in both channels is 63.31\%. All
four improvements remain significant after Holm correction
($p_{\mathrm{Holm}}=.0004$).

Compared with Shared expansion, DESA improves macro nDCG by 1.61\% and Recall

are also nonsignificant. DESA therefore provides a significant nDCG advantage
over Shared expansion.

NFCorpus illustrates the effect of sparse anchoring. Shared expansion increases
the mean sparse support from 561.5 to 3211.6 documents and raises the sparse
stopping depth by 105.81\% relative to Original. DESA preserves the original
support size and reduces sparse depth by 23.15\%, while also reducing dense
depth by 15.03\%. Anchoring allows generated terms to improve the ordering of
originally matched documents without expanding the lexical support.

The $2\times2$ experiment shows that the two channel operators are
complementary. Dense residual expansion alone improves nDCG and Recall by
1.08\% and 1.11\%, with dense and sparse depth reductions of 8.16\% and 7.49\%.
Sparse anchoring alone produces larger effectiveness gains of 3.11\% and 2.01\%,
together with depth reductions of 28.77\% and 27.74\%. Combining both operators
gives the full DESA gains of 3.82\% and 2.38\%, while reducing dense and sparse
depth by 36.90\% and 36.56\%.

Sparse anchoring contributes most of the effectiveness gain, while dense residual
expansion further reduces access depth. Their isolated controls produce only
small differences, whereas the complete channel-asymmetric construction
significantly outperforms Shared expansion in nDCG. DESA's advantage therefore
comes from combining the two channel-specific operations.

Table~\ref{tab:channel-evidence} connects the two operators to their
channel-specific outcomes on each dataset. DESA improves nDCG@10 over Original
on all seven datasets. Adding sparse anchoring to Dense only improves all seven,
while adding dense residual expansion to Sparse only improves six; FiQA is the
sole exception at $-0.66\%$. Under DESA, both replay stopping depths decrease on every
dataset. Shared expansion broadens the positive-score sparse support by up to
$5.72\times$, whereas DESA retains the Original support exactly on six datasets
and by 99.9999\% on Touch\'e-2020, where the exported tail omits 25 extremely
low-scoring document occurrences.

The cross-dataset pattern makes the division of labor explicit: sparse
anchoring supplies the more consistent effectiveness gain, while dense
residual expansion adds complementary gains on most datasets. Together, they
improve retrieval without broadening sparse support and reduce the ranked
evidence required by both channels. Detailed angle, turnover, relevant-rank,
and operator-control diagnostics are reported in
Appendix~\ref{sec:appendix-mechanism}.

Additional binary-mask, reference-count, and operator-control results are
reported in Appendix~\ref{sec:appendix-analysis}.

\subsection{Comparison with Recent and Prior Methods}

Under matched generated references, QuDAR-simple RRF obtains 0.4761 nDCG and
0.4714 Recall, compared with 0.4747 and 0.4695 for DESA. Neither paired difference
is significant after correction. After accounting for finite sparse lists,
QuDAR-simple fuses a mean of 3,911.5 available entries across its four
top-1000-capped rankings. DESA requires a mean of 1,402.1 entries to certify its
fused result under the replay policy, a 64.16\% reduction in equal-dataset mean
entry count (Table~\ref{tab:fidelity}).

Across all seven datasets, DESA's macro nDCG and Recall exceed HyDE's by
15.39\% and 7.32\%. Averaging percentage reductions within datasets, its dense
and sparse stopping depths are 80.48\% and 80.26\% lower. Relative to MuGI, the
macro effectiveness gains are 3.56\% and 2.15\%, and the corresponding depth
reductions are 24.36\% and 37.21\%. These comparisons show higher mean
effectiveness and lower mean access depths.

\subsection{Robustness and Failure Boundary}

DESA retains its effectiveness gains across the tested generator, dense encoder, and
corpus sizes. Access depth decreases in most settings, with Contriever on
Touch\'e-2020 as the only observed failure case. Detailed generator, encoder,
and corpus-scale results are reported in Appendix~\ref{sec:additional-results}.
