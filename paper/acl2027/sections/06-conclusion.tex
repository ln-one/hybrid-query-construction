\section{Conclusion}
\label{sec:conclusion}

A fixed top-$L$ cutoff limits how much of each channel ranking enters fusion
and can change the measured gains and ordering of query expansion methods. We
instead evaluate retrieval effectiveness with complete-list fusion and record
the policy-specific per-channel replay depths at which the fused result is
certified.
DESA uses an LLM to generate complementary reference passages for
channel-asymmetric query expansion.
Orthogonal residual expansion adds their new semantic directions to the dense
query, while score-product anchoring incorporates their lexical evidence
through a document-wise score product without broadening the original query's
lexical support.

Across seven evaluation datasets, DESA improves nDCG@10 and Recall@20
over the unexpanded query by 3.82\% and 2.38\%, with dense and sparse
access-depth reductions of 36.90\% and 36.56\%. With datasets weighted equally,
63.31\% of queries become shallower in both channels. This access benefit is
not universal: depths increase with Contriever on Touch\'e-2020, and the
reductions are non-monotonic across corpus sizes. Query expansion for hybrid
retrieval should therefore integrate generated evidence according to each
channel's matching mechanism and evaluate retrieval effectiveness together
with access depth.
