\section*{Limitations}

Our evaluation covers seven English BEIR datasets, two generators, two
dense encoders, BM25, and equal-weight RRF. The results may differ for other
languages, learned sparse retrievers, or fusion rules. Score-product anchoring
further assumes a non-negative sparse scoring function.

Replay stopping depth measures the ranked evidence needed to certify the fused
top-$K$, rather than wall-clock latency. End-to-end efficiency also depends on
generation, encoding, indexing, and the retrieval interface. Generated
references may contain irrelevant or unsupported expressions, while pooled
relevance judgments may omit relevant documents outside existing pools.
Evaluating DESA with broader retrieval architectures and online incremental
retrieval would clarify these remaining boundaries.
